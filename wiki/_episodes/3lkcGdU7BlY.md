## Summary
Bruce Daisley, former VP of [[organizations/twitter]] Europe, draws on research and his eight-year tenure to argue that workplace joy comes from [[concepts/community]], [[concepts/control]], and [[concepts/creativity]]—not perks or pay. He explores how [[concepts/ego-depletion]], [[concepts/loneliness]], and loss of autonomy fuel [[concepts/burnout]], and why [[concepts/remote-working]] risks eroding the [[concepts/human-connection]] essential for engagement.

## Key Claims
- [[concepts/burnout]] is driven more by lack of [[concepts/control]] than by workload (12:21–12:45)
- [[concepts/positive-affect]] and [[concepts/negative-affect]] are independent dimensions, not opposites (10:10)
- [[concepts/ego-depletion]] explains why decision fatigue erodes willpower throughout the day (14:47)
- [[concepts/community]] and [[concepts/human-connection]] are the strongest predictors of workplace satisfaction (21:08–21:35)
- [[concepts/loneliness]] and [[concepts/isolation]] are epidemic in modern work, especially remote (23:45)
- [[concepts/creativity]] requires switching between the [[concepts/executive-attention-network]], [[concepts/salience-network]], and [[concepts/default-network]] (31:24)
- [[concepts/disengagement]] at work is a bigger problem than active dissatisfaction (38:47–39:11)
- [[concepts/childhood-trauma]] is common among high achievers and can drive obsessive pursuit of success (49:46–51:43)
- Reducing micro-decisions conserves energy; cites [[people/albert-einstein]]'s same-outfit habit and [[people/barack-obama]]'s decision aide (17:12–17:25)
- [[concepts/social-media]] platforms amplify [[concepts/stress]] and [[concepts/fear]] rather than connection (57:42–59:39)
- [[concepts/responsibility]] and [[concepts/purpose]] are essential antidotes to workplace malaise (56:00, 46:49)
